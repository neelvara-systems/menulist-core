If I were in your position, here are the top 3 areas I would focus on next to drive meaningful growth.

1. Double Down on Content for Your Two Audiences
   Your business has two distinct customers: Business Owners (B2C) and Developers (B2B). Your SEO strategy should reflect this. The goal is to become the go-to resource for both.

Action Plan:

Create Dedicated Landing Pages: Right now, you have sections for both audiences on one page. Take it a step further. Create dedicated, indexable pages like /for-restaurants and /for-developers. This allows you to:
Target very specific keywords (e.g., "restaurant menu software" vs. "menu data API").
Tailor the messaging and proof points for each audience without compromise.
Start a Blog with Two Categories: A blog is your most powerful SEO tool. Write articles that solve your customers' problems.
For Business Owners: "5 Ways a Digital Menu Increases Sales," "How to Create a QR Code Menu in 3 Steps," "The Best Menu Designs for Cafes."
For Developers: "How to Scrape Restaurant Data (And Why Our API is Better)," "Building a Food Delivery App with a Menu API," "A Guide to Structured JSON for Menus." 2. Expand Your Structured Data (Schema)
You've already implemented SoftwareApplication schema, which is great. Now, let's add more to get richer results in Google.

Action Plan:

FAQ Schema: On your /pricing page (and any other page with an FAQ section), implement FAQPage schema. This can make your questions and answers appear directly in the search results, taking up more space and increasing clicks.
Article Schema: For every blog post you write, use Article or BlogPosting schema. This helps Google understand your content is editorial and can help it appear in "Top Stories" or other special carousels.
How-To Schema: For guides like "How to Create a QR Code Menu," use HowTo schema. This can result in a rich snippet that shows the step-by-step process directly in Google. 3. Optimize On-Page SEO Fundamentals
With your content strategy in place, ensure every page is technically optimized to rank for its target keywords.

Action Plan:

Image SEO: Ensure every single image on your site uses the Next.js <Image> component and has a descriptive alt tag (e.g., alt="A modern digital menu for a coffee shop displayed on a tablet" instead of alt="menu"). This is crucial for accessibility and ranking in Google Images.
Internal Linking: As you build out your blog and dedicated landing pages, link between them. For example, in a blog post about "Why digital menus are essential," you should link to your main features page and your pricing page. This helps distribute "SEO authority" throughout your site.
Page Speed: Continuously monitor your page load times using Google PageSpeed Insights. Fast-loading sites rank better and provide a better user experience. Since you're on Next.js, you're already ahead, but always be mindful of large images or heavy scripts.
By focusing on these three areas—targeted content, advanced schema, and on-page fundamentals—you'll build a powerful, long-term SEO engine that attracts both of your key customer types and establishes MenuListAI as a leader in the space.

## 1. Executive Summary

MenuList needs a complete launch and product-marketing video system because the product must be understood correctly from the first impression.

The video system exists to make one idea unmistakable:

```text
One approved customer link for your menu, services, and business details.
```

These 12 videos work together as one campaign:

- The 75-second hero film tells the full MenuList story for the website, launch post, LinkedIn, YouTube, sales deck, and investor/sales conversations.
- The 2-3 minute product demo proves the actual workflow for sales calls, onboarding, YouTube, and website education.
- The 30-second launch cut compresses the hero story for paid ads, social launch posts, and quick retargeting.
- The Old PDF, QR Stale Page, Photo/PDF, Owner Approval, One Link Everywhere, and AI Menu Manager reels create fast short-form awareness and objection handling.
- The Official Business Page and Multi-location reels show that MenuList is broader than a QR menu and useful for SMB public-business truth.
- The Founder / Brand POV video gives the launch a low-hype founder explanation that can travel on LinkedIn, Instagram, YouTube, and founder-led sales.

The campaign sequence should make owners feel the scattered-list problem before asking them to act. First show the pain: old PDFs, stale QR pages, price drift, and scattered customer links. Then show the product path: current list, private preview, owner approval, one approved customer link. Then repeat the proof through specific surfaces: QR, Official Business Page, print files, customer actions, AI Menu Manager approval cards, and multi-location governance.

The design team should understand five things before starting:

1. MenuList is not just a QR menu builder.
2. MenuList is not generic AI restaurant software.
3. MenuList is not a flashy SaaS dashboard.
4. Internally, MenuList is public-business truth infrastructure for SMBs; owner-facing video translates this into one owner-approved customer link for the latest published version and never renders the infrastructure phrase.
5. Every video must feel calm, practical, owner-focused, and product-backed.

### AI-Era Market Layer

The 2026 market adds one more strategic layer:

```text
One approved customer link - ready for customers, search, and AI-era discovery.
```

Use this as a market-facing expansion of the core line, not a replacement. The core product promise remains:

```text
One approved customer link for your menu, services, and business details.
```

This AI-era layer means MenuList should look like the approved public source that customers, search, social sharing, QR, print, and AI-era discovery can read more reliably than old PDFs, screenshots, and scattered links. It does not mean MenuList guarantees Google ranking, AI recommendations, AI citations, or traffic.

Approved AI-era line:

```text
Make your business easy to understand for customers, search, and AI-era discovery.
```

Use this carefully. Keep it as readiness/source-quality language, not outcome guarantees.

### Conversion Production Rule

Every master, cutdown, native aspect-ratio version, hook variant, website embed, sales share, and paid ad must have:

1. one funnel stage;
2. one owner belief change;
3. one visible product proof moment;
4. one display close;
5. one linked action and destination;
6. one asset id and `utm_content` value;
7. one primary metric and one quality guard metric;
8. an explicit paid-eligibility decision.

Do not judge the campaign by views alone. The intended business outcome is an owner-approved customer link, with private-preview and approval progress used to distinguish qualified starts from curiosity clicks.

## 3. Visual Language

### Overall Style

The visual style is UI-first, calm, and operational. The product should carry the message.

Use:

- MenuList UI screens;
- owner phone and laptop screen;
- real-world SMB context such as counter, table QR, printed menu, WhatsApp, Instagram bio, and customer scanning QR;
- clean typography;
- minimal animation;
- trustworthy pacing;
- clear before/after contrast;
- product screen recordings with readable UI;
- simple device mockups that do not overpower the product.

Avoid:

- overly flashy SaaS effects;
- generic AI robot visuals;
- futuristic neural-network backgrounds;
- random restaurant stock footage that does not support the product story;
- floating dashboards with fake metrics;
- glitch effects;
- dramatic hype cuts;
- cluttered dashboard montages.

### Color Mood

Use the MenuList website/product visual direction as the base. Keep contrast high and surfaces readable.

Color mood:

- clean neutral base;
- dark text on light panels or light text on dark product frames;
- controlled green/blue trust accents where product status needs emphasis;
- warm real-world shots only when they make the SMB environment feel real;
- no neon AI palettes;
- no heavy purple-blue AI gradient dominance.

### Typography Direction

Typography should be clean, large, and mobile-readable.

Hierarchy:

- Hook text: 44-64px equivalent in 9:16 videos.
- Supporting overlay: 28-40px equivalent.
- Small UI label: use only when unavoidable.
- CTA: large, high contrast, one line where possible.

Rules:

- Short overlays only.
- Maximum 7 words per overlay when possible.
- No dense paragraphs inside video frames.
- No negative letter spacing.

### Icon Direction

Use simple, familiar line icons when an icon helps:

- link;
- QR;
- document/PDF;
- phone;
- check;
- clock;
- location;
- message;
- building/location group.

Icons must stay secondary to UI and text. Do not create a heavy icon-story video with little product proof.

### Animation Style

Animation should feel quiet and useful:

- slow push-ins on UI screens;
- soft wipes between before and after;
- subtle highlight rings around buttons or fields;
- tap indicators for mobile;
- cursor movement only when it clarifies the demo;
- simple line path from approved list to QR/page/print;
- gentle state changes for approval and receipt.

Avoid:

- fast spin transitions;
- particle bursts;
- robotic AI scanning effects;
- glitch transitions;
- fireworks or celebratory confetti;
- exaggerated dashboard counters.

### Transition Style

Use:

- cuts for short reels;
- soft crossfade or slide for hero film;
- direct screen transition for product demo;
- split-screen before/after for problem videos;
- same-frame morph from source list to customer link where useful.

### Caption Style

Captions must be:

- burned in for social exports;
- white or off-white text on dark translucent band when footage is mixed;
- sentence case;
- one or two lines maximum;
- positioned above bottom UI chrome for 9:16 videos;
- not blocking product CTAs, QR codes, or approval buttons.

### Product Screen Recording Style

Screen captures should:

- use clean demo data;
- hide browser chrome where practical;
- avoid debug banners and test IDs;
- show clear cursor or tap movement;
- zoom only to make critical UI readable;
- use stable device frames;
- show the approval step clearly when public content changes;
- avoid showing unsupported or unfinished settings.

### Safe Zones For 9:16 Videos

Keep critical text and product actions inside:

- top safe zone: below 160px from top;
- bottom safe zone: above 260px from bottom;
- side safe zone: 80px from left and right edges.

Do not put CTA, captions, QR code, price, or approval button under platform controls.

### AI Fatigue Avoidance

Because AI-generated brand content is becoming common, MenuList videos should look more human and product-real, not more synthetic.

Use:

- founder POV;
- owner phone screens;
- real WhatsApp-style examples;
- customer scanning QR;
- practical product demos;
- before/after public menu pages;
- simple captions.

Avoid:

- AI avatars;
- robotic narrators;
- blue-glow AI dashboards;
- futuristic control rooms;
- generated-looking restaurant footage;
- fake performance graphs.

### CTA Placement Style

CTA appears:

- final 2-4 seconds for reels;
- final 5 seconds for launch cut;
- final 5-8 seconds for hero film;
- lower-third or full-screen end card for demo;
- never as a constantly floating banner over product UI.

Preferred CTA card:

```text
Create customer link
```

Secondary CTAs:

- Start menu preview.
- Put one trusted customer link online.
- Review before publishing.
- Start from your current list.

## 7. Design System For Video

### Typography Hierarchy

| Use | Direction |
| --- | --- |
| Hook | Large, bold, 1-2 lines |
| Overlay | Medium, sentence case |
| Caption | Lower third, readable, max 2 lines |
| CTA | Large, high-contrast, centered or lower third |
| Label | Small but readable, only when required |

### Caption Style

- Burned-in captions for social.
- White text on dark translucent bar.
- Sentence case.
- No more than 2 lines.
- Do not cover UI buttons or QR codes.

### Lower-Third Style

- Use for founder name/title only if needed.
- Keep it small and calm.
- No animated badge clutter.

### CTA Card Style

- Solid calm background.
- MenuList logo.
- One CTA line.
- No more than one secondary note.

### Product Highlight Style

- Soft outline.
- Subtle glow only if needed for contrast.
- Tap/cursor ring for actions.
- No flashing warning colors except for staged before/problem frames.

### Icon Style

- Simple line icons.
- Use only to support text.
- Do not replace product screenshots with icon animations.

### Background Treatment

- Clean neutral backgrounds.
- Product UI on flat or lightly shaded surfaces.
- Real-world footage should feel practical and local, not stock-ad glossy.

### Transition Rules

- Reels: direct cuts and simple wipes.
- Hero film: soft cuts and split-screen before/after.
- Demo: UI-first direct transitions.
- Founder POV: talking head with product cutaways.

### Safe Margins For Vertical Video

- Keep main text within 80px left/right margins.
- Keep top text below 160px.
- Keep CTA above bottom 260px.
- Keep QR away from platform overlays.

### Minimum Font Size For Mobile

- Hook: 44px equivalent minimum.
- Overlay: 32px equivalent minimum.
- Captions: 28px equivalent minimum.
- UI label zooms should be large enough to read on a 390px-wide phone.

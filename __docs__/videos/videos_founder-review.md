## What Is Approved

### 1. Category Positioning

Approved.

MenuList should be positioned as public-business truth infrastructure for SMBs, not as:

- a QR menu maker;
- a generic AI restaurant tool;
- a flashy SaaS dashboard;
- a website builder;
- a ranking or traffic product.

Evidence:

- The master blueprint tells the design team that MenuList is not just a QR builder, not generic AI restaurant software, not a flashy SaaS dashboard, and is public-business truth infrastructure.
- The videos index makes the same source-of-truth statement for the full folder.

### 2. Core Product Line

Approved.

Use as the permanent product line:

```text
One approved customer link for your menu, services, and business details.
```

This line is clear, broad enough for restaurants and service businesses, and concrete enough for SMB owners.

### 3. AI-Era Market Line

Approved with strict boundaries.

Use as a market-facing expansion:

```text
One approved customer link - ready for customers, search, and AI-era discovery.
```

This line is approved because it connects MenuList to the 2026 discovery reality without claiming ranking, recommendation, or automation outcomes.

Mandatory boundary:

```text
This is readiness/source-quality language, not a ranking, traffic, citation, recommendation, or automatic external-platform update promise.
```

### 4. AI Menu Manager Framing

Approved.

Use this structure everywhere AI appears:

1. Owner says what changed.
2. MenuList prepares the update.
3. Owner reviews the card.
4. Owner approves.
5. Customer link updates.

```text
AI prepares. Owner approves. Customers see the current version.
```

Do not show uncontrolled automation, robots, glowing AI brains, AI avatars, autonomous publishing, or external platform updates.

## Founder Findings

### Finding 1 - The Strategy Is Correct

Severity: none.

The video system now protects the moat: MenuList is the approved public source behind customer-facing surfaces.

The strongest line is still the simple product line:

```text
One approved customer link for your menu, services, and business details.
```

The AI-era line should support that line, not replace it.

### Finding 2 - The AI-Era Line Is Powerful But Easy To Misuse

Severity: medium risk if not controlled in production.

The phrase "ready for customers, search, and AI-era discovery" is strategically useful, but editors, ad buyers, or copywriters could accidentally turn it into a visibility promise.

Required mitigation:

- keep the readiness/source-quality boundary in every production brief;
- do not use ranking-style visuals;
- do not show Google, ChatGPT, Instagram, or review platforms as if MenuList updates them;
- use generic discovery-system visuals if needed;
- run final claim review before publishing any paid ad.

### Finding 3 - The Product Must Stay Visibly Owner-Controlled

Severity: high if missed in AI-related videos.

Every AI-related frame must show the control path:

```text
message -> prepared card -> owner review -> owner approval -> customer version
```

If the viewer thinks AI silently edits the public menu, the video fails.

### Finding 4 - Production Quality Must Be Practical, Not Cinematic Hype

Severity: medium.

MenuList will look more trustworthy if it shows real phone flows, product UI, QR/table context, owner messages, and customer pages.

Avoid startup-ad polish that makes the product feel generic. The design should feel calm, local, current, and useful.

### Finding 5 - Public Publishing Is Still Blocked

Severity: launch blocker.

The docs are ready, but public video publishing is not ready until the team has:

- final or clearly labeled demo UI captures;
- approved fictional or permissioned demo business data;
- final CTA destination;
- founder voiceover or talking-head decision;
- export specs confirmed by channel;
- final claim-boundary review;
- campaign tracking decision.

## Required Production Gates

### Gate 1 - Script Freeze

Before recording:

- confirm the final voiceover for each of the 12 videos;
- remove any accidental ranking, traffic, sales, or external-platform wording;
- confirm all CTAs use approved lines such as `Create customer link`, `Start menu preview`, or `Put one trusted customer link online`.

### Gate 2 - Demo Data Approval

Before screen capture:

- use fictional demo businesses unless a real customer has granted permission;
- label mock/demo visuals where they could be mistaken for customer proof;
- verify menu/service prices, hours, and item states are internally consistent.

### Gate 3 - UI Capture Approval

Before editing:

- capture upload/source intake;
- capture private preview;
- capture owner approval;
- capture customer link;
- capture QR/table/print surfaces;
- capture Official Business Page;
- capture AI Menu Manager approval card;
- capture multi-location only from approved demo data.

### Gate 4 - First Edit Review

Before final exports:

- check mobile text readability;
- verify no UI is covered by captions;
- confirm the product appears early enough;
- confirm AI visuals stay grounded in product UI;
- confirm external surfaces are shown as owner-placed links.

### Gate 5 - Final Claim Review

Before public posting or paid ads:

- no ranking claims;
- no AI recommendation claims;
- no guaranteed outcomes;
- no external automatic update claims;
- no fake testimonials or metrics;
- no unsupported integrations.

### Gate 6 - Distribution Readiness

Before launch sequence:

- confirm website placement;
- confirm upload destination for YouTube/LinkedIn/Instagram;
- confirm paid ad tracking;
- confirm WhatsApp sales version;
- confirm who responds to inbound leads;
- confirm launch-day owner/founder availability.

## Founder Notes To The Team

Use the product as the hero.

Do not over-animate. Do not make MenuList look like an AI chatbot, QR generator, or restaurant SaaS dashboard. The strongest proof is a current approved list becoming one trusted customer link, then supporting QR, page, print, customer actions, and controlled updates.

If a line sounds like it promises visibility, revenue, ranking, or automation, remove it.

If a scene makes the owner look like they lost control, change it.

If a frame does not show product truth or real owner/customer context, cut it.

# MenuList Owner Ease 30s V4 Landscape Brand Type - HyperFrames Design

## Style Prompt

Calm operational product video for MenuList focused on non-technical SMB owners. The V4 direction should feel more premium and cinematic than V3 while staying practical: upload existing menu photos or a PDF, get a private preview, review before publishing, then use one approved customer link. Keep the product truth as the hero. Borrow only high-level motion-card principles from SaaS references: layered cards, tilted surfaces, cursor-led transformation, clear foreground metadata. Avoid direct copying, AI hype, neon SaaS styling, robot imagery, revenue charts, fake testimonials, and external-platform automation visuals.

This project uses the real MenuList website theme and supplied MenuList symbol, not the earlier temporary green draft theme. The final lockup must show the plain transparent symbol and MenuList name directly; do not wrap the logo in a square app tile, add a faded duplicate, or place the logo inside a separate card.

## Typography

- Use the repo-local Inter variable font across headlines, body copy, UI, labels, captions, CTA text, and the final lockup.
- Use zero letter spacing everywhere.
- Use H1 `1.08`, H2 `1.12`, body `1.5`, and caption `1.35` line heights.
- Use the exact website gradient `linear-gradient(90deg, #0051d1 0%, #0284c7 52%, #27a8e3 100%)` on one meaningful phrase per scene while the remaining headline stays dark.
- Follow [the founder-approved video production standard](../../videos_founder-approved-production-standard.md).

## Motion

- Pacing: calm and deliberate.
- Transitions: cream paper wipes and UI slides, no glitch effects.
- Entrances: short 0.35-0.7s movements with varied easing.
- Highlights: soft outlines, cursor movement, tap rings, small zoom cues, and check states.
- Captions: large, sentence-case, two lines maximum.
- Background words: scene-specific only, animated letter-by-letter with very low opacity, and never used as the main readable claim.
- Owner-ease rule: every scene should reduce the perceived setup effort, never add a new technical task.
- V4 frame rule: every frame must show either an owner action, private preview, approval state, or approved-link outcome. Avoid empty decorative space.
- Glass rule: use subtle translucent surfaces and soft shadows only. Do not turn the video into a neon/futuristic AI SaaS visual.
- Device rule: mobile frames should feel light and native. Avoid heavy black bezels unless showing a real captured phone.
- Card rule: cards must feel like product artifacts with video-visible borders, clear labels, and realistic shadows. Equivalent source options use a consistent grid; varied hierarchy is reserved for unequal proof surfaces.
- Public-frame metadata rule: never expose production labels such as scene names, beat names, or internal workflow annotations.
- Lower-third rule: do not add a summary card when the headline, body, or proof UI already communicates the same claim. A lower-third is allowed only for unique necessary information and must remain outside decorative typography and proof surfaces.
- Identity rule: use a small plain MenuList watermark at bottom-right during scenes, not a top-left header-style brand card.
- Slate lockup rule: use a two-column layout with the plain symbol on the left and a right-side copy stack. `MenuList` is row one, `One approved customer link` is row two, and both text rows share the exact same left edge.
- Animated-mark rule: reuse the exact icon-only path geometry from `src/components/atoms/animatedVerticalLogo/`. At both slates, begin with a faint complete scaffold, genuinely draw both gradient component paths once from their dash offsets, follow with one lighter trace pass, then settle to the complete mark. Use approximately `1.0-1.05s` for the real draw and `0.88s` for the trace. Never loop.
- Frozen-symbol rule: the founder has approved the symbol animation as final. Future wordmark, tagline, slate, or audio iterations must not alter the two logo paths, scaffold, gradient draw, trace, geometry, or timing.
- Wordmark-motion rule: keep a readable faint `MenuList` scaffold for frame-zero safety, then reveal the eight foreground characters in strict left-to-right order through a soft horizontal mask, short blur settle, and restrained blue-to-ink color settle. Use one quiet gradient baseline sweep that disappears before the final static hold. Do not scramble, rearrange, rotate, bounce, or add a typewriter cursor.
- Poster-frame rule: encoded frame zero must already show the large plain MenuList symbol, MenuList name, and `One approved customer link`; never begin from an empty background or wait for a delayed reveal.
- Director polish rule: apply the motion, focus, transition, logo, and audio checklist in the founder-approved production standard before rendering. Every cue must explain the owner workflow instead of decorating the frame.

## What NOT To Do

- Do not use gradients as the main visual idea.
- Do not use repeated blue background grids or bands.
- Do not use robots, AI brains, blue glow, or futuristic dashboards.
- Do not show Google, Instagram, Zomato, Swiggy, or delivery platforms as if MenuList updates them.
- Do not use sales lift, ranking, traffic, or AI recommendation claims.
- Do not make the UI feel like a generic SaaS dashboard.

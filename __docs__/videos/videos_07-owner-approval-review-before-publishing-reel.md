## Shared Positioning

MenuList must be shown as public-business truth infrastructure for SMBs, not as a standalone QR menu app, generic AI restaurant software, or flashy SaaS dashboard.

Core line:

```text
One approved customer link for your menu, services, and business details.
```

AI-era market line:

```text
One approved customer link - ready for customers, search, and AI-era discovery.
```

Use this as readiness/source-quality language only. Do not turn it into a ranking, traffic, AI recommendation, or external-platform update claim.

Required boundaries:

- Show owner approval before public customer-facing changes.
- Keep external surfaces framed as owner-placed links, not automatic external-platform updates.
- Avoid fake testimonials, fake numbers, fake customer logos, ranking claims, growth claims, and AI magic visuals.
- Use fictional or permissioned demo data only.

## Video 7 - Owner Approval / Review Before Publishing Reel

### A. Purpose

This trust-focused reel proves that MenuList does not silently publish public content.

### B. Audience

Skeptical owners, owners worried about wrong prices, owners worried about AI or automation, setup partners, sales prospects.

### C. Placement

- Instagram Reel;
- YouTube Short;
- TikTok;
- LinkedIn;
- WhatsApp sales;
- onboarding;
- paid retargeting.

### D. Recommended Duration

- Master: 20-30 seconds.
- 15-second trust cut.
- 9:16 required.
- 1:1 optional.

### E. Core Message

Nothing goes public until the owner approves the customer-facing version.

### F. Emotional Job

The viewer should feel: "MenuList gives me safety without extra public risk."

### G. Narrative Arc

1. Hook: Nothing goes public until you approve.
2. Problem: Owners fear silent changes.
3. Product entry: Prepared private preview.
4. Proof: Owner checks item, category, price.
5. Outcome: Customer link updates after approval.
6. CTA: Review before publishing.

### I. Full Voiceover Script

```text
Nothing goes public until you approve.

MenuList prepares the customer-facing version first.

You check the important parts: item, price, category, and details.

Then you approve what customers will see.

The customer link updates only after approval.
```

### J. On-Screen Text List

1. Nothing goes public until you approve.
2. Private preview first.
3. Check item. Price. Category.
4. Owner approval.
5. Customer link updated.
6. Review before publishing.

### K. UI Screens Required

- private preview screen;
- item/category/price review screen;
- owner approval screen;
- customer link updated screen.

### L. Motion Design Direction

Use a calm lock/unlock metaphor only around publication, not security hype. Use tap indicators and field highlights. Keep public page hidden until approval is shown.

### M. Sound Direction

Use quiet music. Add a soft click for approval. Use a short silence or reduced music bed right before "Owner approval" to emphasize safety.

### N. Design Checklist

- The word "private" or "not public" appears before approval.
- Approval is shown as owner action.
- Customer page appears after approval.
- No AI automation visuals appear.
- Text fits in vertical safe zone.

### O. Versioning Requirements

- 9:16 captioned.
- 9:16 clean.
- 1:1 captioned.
- 15-second trust cut.
- With VO.
- Without VO.
- Thumbnail frame.

### P. CTA

Review before publishing.

### Q. Conversion Contract

- Funnel stage: trust.
- Belief change: `MenuList prepares the work, but nothing customer-facing goes live without my review.`
- Proof moment: prepared private preview/card -> item/price check -> explicit owner approval -> customer version.
- Display close: `Review before publishing.`
- Linked action: companion `Create customer link` -> `/create-menu`.
- Primary metric: claim/review progress and first approved publish per referred session.
- Guard metric: no autonomous-publishing interpretation.
- Asset/UTM pattern: `approval_v1_owner_control_20s_<aspect>`.
- Paid eligibility: blocked until approval milestones can be attributed and the exact cut is ledger-approved.

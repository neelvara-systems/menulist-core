# Feature Launch - Featured Choices

**Status:** Script-ready; fresh runtime capture required before production
**Primary runtime rows:** `design_presentation` and current eligible-choice behavior
**Campaign authority:** [Feature Launch Inventory](./feature-launch-inventory.md)
**Mandatory standards:** [Founder-Approved Production Standard](../videos_founder-approved-production-standard.md), [HyperFrames Operating Guide](../videos_hyperframes-operating-guide.md), and [Asset Intake And Readiness](../videos_asset-intake-and-readiness.md)

## A. Purpose

Explain how MenuList can give customers a useful starting point inside a long approved menu without making sales, ranking, or personalization claims.

## B. Audience

Owners with large menus or service lists and customers who need a faster first choice.

## C. Placement

- Feature page.
- Instagram Reel and YouTube Short.
- LinkedIn feature post.
- WhatsApp sales.
- Retargeting.
- Product demo.

## D. Recommended Duration

- Master: 35 seconds.
- Vertical cut: 25 seconds.
- Paid cut: 15 seconds.
- Silent customer-page loop: 8 seconds.

## E. Core Message

Featured Choices can surface a few eligible options while the full approved menu and availability rules remain intact.

## F. Emotional Job

`Customers can begin with a clearer choice without me maintaining a separate recommendation list.`

## G. Narrative Arc

1. **Hook:** A long menu can make the first choice harder.
2. **Problem:** Customers must scan too much before finding a useful starting point.
3. **Product entry:** Featured Choices appears before the complete menu.
4. **Proof:** Eligible choices respect current menu and availability state.
5. **Outcome:** The customer starts faster while one menu remains authoritative.
6. **CTA:** Guide the first look. Keep one menu.

## K. UI Screens Required

- Customer page with a credible long menu.
- Current Featured Choices module.
- Current owner-selection or eligible-choice control/state.
- Availability/status fixture.
- Featured Choices after an unavailable item is excluded.
- Customer item detail.
- Full approved menu below the featured module.

## L. Motion Design Direction

- Start with a brief menu scan, then stop on the decision-support module.
- Reveal no more than three featured cards in the first proof scene.
- Keep card motion directional and tied to eligibility or availability.
- Avoid carousel-like perpetual movement.
- Use one expansion to item detail as the main `aha` moment.
- Preserve the full menu in frame so Featured Choices never looks like a separate list.
- Use no-pill captions and avoid covering item names or prices.

## M. Sound Direction

- Use `One Link Motion v2` with voice-reactive ducking.
- Use one soft selection tick per revealed choice, capped at three.
- Use one muted state-change cue when an unavailable item leaves.
- Let the music lift slightly on the item-detail expansion.
- Avoid popularity, jackpot, cash-register, or gamified sounds.

## N. Design Checklist

- [ ] Featured Choices uses current runtime UI and terminology.
- [ ] The full approved menu remains visibly authoritative.
- [ ] The eligibility basis is accurately described.
- [ ] No personal profiling or one-to-one recommendation claim appears.
- [ ] No `best seller`, revenue, conversion, or ranking claim is invented.

- [ ] Cards remain readable in native vertical output.

## Q. Conversion And Claim Contract

- **Conversion job:** Demonstrate customer decision support without turning MenuList into a recommendation engine.
- **Primary proof:** A few eligible choices appear above, and remain connected to, the full approved menu.
- **Allowed:** Current owner-selection, eligible activity, and availability behavior verified in runtime.
- **Required:** Full-menu continuity and owner/source control.
- **Blocked:** Guaranteed sales, best-seller claims without data, personal recommendations, ranking, or promotion of unavailable/unsafe items.
- **Release gate:** Validate eligibility rules, owner controls, availability behavior, and public labels with the current feature implementation.

---

## Feature Narrative (Business Value)

### The Problem Every SMB Owner Faces

Every few months, costs go up. Suppliers raise prices. Seasonal ingredients change. And the owner sits there, updating items one by one — in the POS, on the printed menu, on the QR code, on the screen.

It takes hours. It's stressful. And mistakes happen.

### How MenuList Solves This

MenuList's Menu Command Center lets owners update many items at once:

- Select a category or pick specific items
- Choose how to change (increase %, decrease %, set fixed, show/hide, move)
- See exactly what will change before applying
- Apply in one click through the editor save path

No mistakes. No stress. No double-checking.

### Why This Matters

When pricing changes happen inside MenuList first, the public link and configured screens follow their supported refresh paths. Downloaded PDFs, printed menus, and POS/external systems need their own replacement or connected integration evidence. MenuList becomes where menu decisions happen.

### The Authority Shift

This feature creates a behavioral transition: owners stop opening their POS first and start opening MenuList first. Once bulk pricing happens inside MenuList, authority shifts from POS → MenuList. That’s how infrastructure products win — they become the place where real decisions happen.

After this feature, owners eliminate dependency on:

- Spreadsheets for price calculations
- Designers for menu updates
- POS as the first update point
- Manual item-by-item editing across systems

MenuList becomes the fastest control surface for menu operations.

## Safety as a Sales Differentiator

This feature touches money. Safety matters more than UX.

Position the Command Center’s safety system as **billing-grade reliability**:

- Preview every change before it goes live (no blind updates)
- Hard limits prevent catastrophic pricing errors
- One-click undo within 30 seconds
- Multi-outlet protection — locked items are automatically excluded

This is not a "nice to have" — it’s how owners build trust in the system. If bulk pricing fails once, trust damage is severe and permanent. Zero tolerance for pricing bugs.

Sales framing: "MenuList treats your prices the way banks treat transactions — preview, confirm, and the ability to reverse."

## Extensibility Story (Internal)

The Command Center is a **control surface**, not a single-purpose tool. The architecture supports future actions without rebuilding:

- Tax/GST adjustments
- Tag management
- Promotional pricing
- Attribute-level changes
- Chain-wide controls

All would plug into the same 3-panel modal (Selection → Action → Preview). This is infrastructure thinking — build the control surface once, add capabilities over time.

## Feature Narrative (Business Value)

### The Problem

Every restaurant owner knows the feeling: You check Google Reviews and see a new 1-star rating. The customer had a bad experience, but they never said anything during their visit. Now it's permanent, public, and affecting your business.

**The reality:**
- Most unhappy customers don't complain — they just leave quietly
- By the time it's a Google Review, it's too late
- Owners discover problems from public feedback, not private channels

### The Solution

Guest Feedback creates a **pressure-release valve** — a private channel between your customers and you.

**How it works:**
1. Customer scans a QR code or taps a link on your menu
2. They share their experience (good or bad) directly with you
3. You see it in your private inbox
4. They're then invited to leave a Google Review (your reputation, your way)

### The Outcome

- **Catch issues early** — Before they become public reviews
- **Understand patterns** — See what's working and what's not
- **Protect your reputation** — A private channel reduces public complaints
- **No extra work** — Simple inbox, no learning curve

## Sales Talking Points (Objection Handlers)

### Objection 1: "We already get feedback on Google"

**Response:**
> Google feedback is public and permanent. By the time you see it, the damage is done. Guest Feedback gives customers a private channel first — you hear about problems before Google does.

---

### Objection 2: "Our customers don't complain"

**Response:**
> That's exactly the problem. 96% of unhappy customers never complain — they just leave and never come back. Guest Feedback lowers the barrier: one tap, rate, done. You'll hear from customers who'd never fill out a survey or call a manager.

---

### Objection 3: "We don't have time to check another inbox"

**Response:**
> It's not another tool — it's a tab in your existing MenuList dashboard. No notifications, no alerts. Check it when you have 2 minutes. Most owners check it with their morning coffee.

---

### Objection 4: "What if we get too much negative feedback?"

**Response:**
> That means you're catching problems early — which is exactly what you want. Better to see 10 private complaints than 1 public Google Review. Each complaint is a chance to fix something before it hurts your reputation.

---

### Objection 5: "Can't customers just leave bad feedback and then a bad Google Review?"

**Response:**
> They can. But here's what happens in practice: customers who feel heard are less likely to post publicly. The private channel acts as a pressure release. And even if they do post, you've already seen the feedback — no surprises.

---

### Objection 6: "Is this trying to game Google Reviews?"

**Response:**
> No — and that would be illegal. We show the Google Review invitation to everyone, regardless of their rating. A 1-star and a 5-star customer see the same invitation. We're fully compliant with Google's policies.

## Appendix: Compliance Statement

### Google Review Policy Compliance

MenuList's Guest Feedback feature is designed to be **fully compliant** with Google's Business Profile review policies:

1. **No review gating:** The Google Review invitation is shown to ALL customers, regardless of their feedback rating.

2. **No selective solicitation:** We do not filter, hide, or redirect customers based on sentiment.

3. **Transparency:** Feedback goes directly to the owner — nothing is hidden or manipulated.

4. **Customer choice:** Leaving a Google Review is always optional and clearly presented as such.

**Reference:** [Google Business Profile Review Policy](https://support.google.com/business/answer/2622994)

# Framework 1: Expansion Without Authority Dilution

## Core Law

> **MenuList may expand what it controls, but must never expand what the owner decides.**

- Expansion adds **choices, settings, modes, decisions** → Authority DILUTION ❌
- Expansion adds **coverage, reach, autonomy** → Authority COMPOUNDING ✅

---

## The 4 Allowed Expansion Vectors

### VECTOR 1: Surface Expansion (Same Brain, More Places)

MenuList controls **more surfaces** using the **same intelligence loop**.

**Valid Examples:**

- Google Business Profile menus
- Delivery app menu sync
- In-store kiosk screens
- WhatsApp auto-replies
- Table NFC chips

**Critical Rule:** No surface may introduce surface-specific settings.

- ❌ "Customize Google menu behavior"
- ✅ Same rules. Same silence. Same authority.

### VECTOR 2: Temporal Expansion (Same Decisions, More Time)

MenuList takes responsibility for decisions **earlier, longer, or continuously**.

**Valid Examples:**

- Seasonal transitions handled automatically
- Price drift normalization over months
- Long-term bestseller inertia
- Auto-sunsetting underperforming items

**Critical Rule:** Time-based changes must never be announced.

- ❌ "We adjusted your menu for the season"
- ✅ "Menu state is stable." (or silence)

### VECTOR 3: Contextual Expansion (Same Logic, More Situations)

MenuList adapts behavior based on **context it infers**, not context the owner declares.

**Valid Examples:**

- Weather-aware menu emphasis
- Daypart-aware staff prompts
- Device-type awareness
- Language switching by user locale

**Critical Rule:** Context must never be named.

- ❌ "Because it's raining…"
- ✅ Just behavior. No narration.

### VECTOR 4: Failure Absorption (Same Output, More Resilience)

MenuList expands by absorbing more failure modes **before the owner ever sees them**.

**Valid Examples:**

- Bad data → fallback silently
- API outage → cached state
- Low confidence → suppression
- Screen failure → brand slide

**Critical Rule:** Failures must degrade into silence, not messaging.

- ❌ "We encountered an issue…"
- ✅ "Everything is running normally." (or nothing)

---

## The Expansion Kill-List (ABSOLUTE NO)

Instant rejection if proposal includes:

- New dashboards
- New toggles
- New "modes"
- Advanced settings
- Conditional rules
- Explanations
- Visibility into reasoning
- Performance breakdowns
- Comparisons
- "Power user" access

**These are authority leaks, not features.**

---

## The Expansion Test

> **After this ships, will the owner feel more or less responsible for outcomes?**

- More responsible → **KILL IT**
- Same responsibility → Neutral, reconsider
- Less responsible → **GREENLIGHT**

# Framework 2: Category Positioning Without Explanation

## Core Law

> **MenuList must be understood emotionally, not intellectually.**

The moment someone can _describe_ MenuList accurately, they start _evaluating_ it.
Evaluation → comparison → supervision → erosion.

---

## Categories MenuList is NOT In

MenuList must **never** be framed as:

- ❌ AI tool
- ❌ Menu software
- ❌ Marketing platform
- ❌ Growth tool
- ❌ Optimization engine
- ❌ Analytics system
- ❌ Assistant
- ❌ Recommendation engine

---

## The Only Category That Works

MenuList is not a _tool category_. It is a **behavioral category**.

**Correct Mental Slot:**

> "This runs my menu."

Not:

- "This helps me manage…"
- "This improves…"
- "This gives insights…"

Just: **Runs.**

---

## External Positioning Rule

Externally, MenuList must be **underspecified**.

- Clear explanation → invites comparison
- Comparison → invites skepticism
- Skepticism → invites monitoring

**Underspecification ends the conversation early.**

---

## Approved Public Descriptions (Maximum Length)

- "MenuList runs your menu automatically."
- "MenuList handles menu behavior."
- "MenuList is always on."
- "No setup. No tuning."
- "No action needed."

**Anything longer is wrong.**

---

## The Category Test

> **Does this make MenuList feel more like software, or more like a utility?**

- Software → reject
- Utility → keep

# Framework 3: Distribution Without Education

## Core Law

> **If distribution requires understanding, the product is not ready to spread.**

Goal is not adoption through comprehension.
Goal is adoption through **relief**.

---

## Why Education Breaks Authority

Education causes:

1. **Curiosity**
2. **Evaluation**
3. **Supervision**

Once someone learns _how_ something works, they feel entitled to question, adjust, override, blame themselves.

---

## The 4 Allowed Distribution Channels

### CHANNEL 1: Physical Trigger Distribution (Primary)

MenuList spreads through **objects**, not explanations.

- QR cards, table tents, stickers, NFC tags, screens, printed menus

**Critical Rule:** The physical surface must work even if the owner says nothing.

### CHANNEL 2: Staff Transmission (Silent Virality)

**Correct Pattern — Staff say:**

- "That's just how the menu works."
- "It updates automatically."
- "We don't control it."

**Critical Rule:** Staff must never explain MenuList — only defer to it.

### CHANNEL 3: Embedded Default Distribution

MenuList spreads by being pre-installed, default-on, bundled, assumed.

> The owner doesn't _choose_ MenuList — they inherit it.
> **Inheritance > Decision**

### CHANNEL 4: Outcome-Based Referral (No Storytelling)

**Acceptable:** "We don't manage menus anymore." "It just runs."
**Forbidden:** "It boosted sales" "It's really smart"

The more boring the referral, the faster trust transfers.

---

## The Anti-Channels (NEVER Use)

Instant rejection:

- Content marketing explaining MenuList
- Thought leadership
- Educational blogs
- AI explainers
- Feature walkthrough videos
- Comparison pages
- Case studies
- "How MenuList works" pages
- Long sales decks

---

## Onboarding = Installation, Not Journey

**Correct:**

1. Menu appears
2. "This is set."
3. Silence

**Wrong:**

1. Step 1 of 7
2. Configure
3. Explain
4. Confirm
5. Customize

**That's a SaaS death spiral.**

---

## The Distribution Test

> **Can this spread if nobody explains anything?**

- Yes → allowed
- No → reject

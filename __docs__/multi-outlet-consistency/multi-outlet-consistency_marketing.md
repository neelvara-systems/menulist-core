## 3. Feature Narrative (Business Value)

### The Real Problem Isn't Menus. It's Consistency.

When you have multiple outlets, menu drift is guaranteed:

- The same item is priced differently across locations
- Items disappear randomly at certain outlets
- Staff edits the menu without HQ knowing
- Customers see different menus at different locations

This creates a recurring burden for owners:

> "Is every outlet showing the same brand menu?"

### How MenuList Solves This

MenuList gives you **one master menu** that controls every outlet. Update once, update everywhere.

But we understand each location is different. So outlets can still:

- Adjust prices for local costs
- Mark items as unavailable (sold out)
- Add local specials like "Chef's Special"
- Reorder categories for their flow

**The brand stays protected. Local flexibility stays possible.**

## 9. Sales Talking Points (Objection Handlers)

### Objection: "Each outlet is different."

**Response:**

> "That's normal. You can do local pricing and local specials. The master menu just keeps the brand consistent. You control what can be changed locally."

### Objection: "My manager already handles this."

**Response:**

> "Managers can update menus. They can't prevent drift across locations. This removes the need to check every week."

### Objection: "Will it overwrite our outlet menu?"

**Response:**

> "No. Your outlet keeps its local items and overrides. The master only controls brand content—and you decide what that means."

### Objection: "Is this complicated to set up?"

**Response:**

> "No. You connect your outlets once, and the system stays aligned. We help with migration if you have existing menus."

### Objection: "We already use [competitor]."

**Response:**

> "Most POS systems don't offer menu consistency across outlets. MenuList focuses specifically on keeping your brand menu aligned—it works alongside your existing tools."

### Objection: "Will this really help our sales?"

**Response:**

> "This isn't about sales optimization. It's about trust. Customers notice when prices don't match. This removes that anxiety—for you and for them."

## 11. Pricing/Packaging Story

### How to Position Cost/Value

**Value Framing:**

- "How many hours do you spend syncing menus every week?"
- "What's the cost of one customer complaint about wrong prices?"
- "What's your time worth?"

**Pricing Approach:**

- Multi-outlet is a **premium tier feature**
- Requires 2+ outlets to activate
- **Quantity-based pricing:** Same plan price × number of stores (via Razorpay subscription quantity)
- Adding an outlet = prorated charge for the current billing cycle
- Deactivating = quantity reduced immediately
- Value: Saves 2+ hours/week, prevents brand damage

**Upgrade Trigger:**

Sell Multi-Outlet when customer:

- Has 2+ locations
- Mentions inconsistency complaints
- Relies on managers to update menus
- Runs the same menu across branches
- Asks about "syncing" or "copying" menus
